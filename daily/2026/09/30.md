### [📄 Third Circuit rejects "fair use" for AI training in Thomson Reuters v. ROSS — first US appellate ruling](https://www.law360.com/articles/2531563/3rd-circ-affirms-thomson-reuters-westlaw-ai-copyright-win)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

A Third Circuit panel (Judge Montgomery-Reeves) on Sept 29 affirmed the February 2025 summary judgment that ROSS Intelligence's copying of thousands of Westlaw headnotes to train an AI legal-search tool was **not fair use**. It is the **first US federal appellate decision** on whether AI training can be fair use of copyrighted material. The court's full reasoning is under seal (Westlaw's headnotes sit behind a paywall).

**Why it matters:** An appellate-level "no" on training-data fair use stiffens the legal risk around scraped corpora — and by extension the provenance/poisoning story Manny tracks in model supply chains.

### [📄 New Spectre v2 variant "Branch Target Reuse" leaks data from Intel, AMD and Arm CPUs](https://www.securityweek.com/new-spectre-v2-variant-exposes-intel-amd-arm-cpus-to-data-leaks/)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

VUSec (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam) and Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna disclosed **Branch Target Reuse (BTR)**, a Spectre v2 variant targeting JIT compilers in kernels, browsers and runtimes. Stale indirect-branch predictions survive self-modifying code and get reused, yielding a **speculative execute-after-free** primitive. Two end-to-end Linux kernel exploits (via unprivileged cBPF, still used by seccomp, Docker and Chrome) leaked the **root password hash** at ~8 bytes/sec and bypassed all enabled mitigations on modern Intel CPUs; SpiderMonkey (Firefox) and GraalVM PoCs also landed.

**Why it matters:** "Fully patched, default settings, still leaking" is the hardest kind of vulnerability to communicate — prime material for a hardware-mitigation-myth explainer.

### [📄 Rig Security emerges from stealth with $12M to separate rogue agents from real users](https://www.securityweek.com/rig-security-emerges-from-stealth-with-12m-to-tackle-agentic-ai-identity-risks/)
**⏱ Read time:** 2 min

Tel Aviv–based **Rig Security** launched with **$12M** (Ten Eleven Ventures, Brightmind, CrowdStrike Falcon Fund) and an identity-dependency graph for the agentic era. The problem it targets: agents inherit the identity of the developer or service that spawned them, so a SIEM can't tell a human action from a subverted agent's — and the standard fix (disable the account) also disables the employee. Rig's bet is graph-based attribution of user-vs-agent actions, echoing founder Guy Kozliner's Wiz background.

**Why it matters:** "Agent acts with the user's full permissions, at machine speed, invisibly" is the core detection gap — expect this attribution layer to become a category.

### [📄 ToolFence: Fine-Grained Authorization for Secure Tool-Using LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37196)
**⏱ Read time:** 2 min
**📄 Full breakdown:** [papers/2026/09/toolfence-fine-grained-authorization-tool-using-agents.md](../../papers/2026/09/toolfence-fine-grained-authorization-tool-using-agents.md)

ToolFence compiles a typed **authorization blueprint** before execution and enforces it with a deterministic monitor, asking a judge for new capabilities only when the blueprint is incomplete. Because it authorizes *effects* with provenance — distinguishing user-authorized values from untrusted observations — it addresses the **within-tool attack** (legitimate tool, attacker-chosen arguments) that content-based defenses miss. On AgentDojo with Qwen3-max it drives attack success to near zero with a 3.80-pt clean-utility drop.

**Why it matters:** Provenance-based effect authorization is the clearest path past the "filter the text" dead end — a design Manny can cite against consensus-filtering hype.

### [CounterSteer: Suppressing Indirect Prompt Injection with Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36570)
**Authors:** Mark Russinovich | **Published:** 2026-09-29

An inference-time defense that subtracts a residual-stream direction from tool-result tokens during prefill — always-on, no fine-tuning, no auxiliary model. Held-out attack success drops from 0.21–1.00 to 0.00–0.17 across five open-weights models, with AgentDojo compromise falling to 0.006–0.079. Caveat: parameter manipulation (attacker-chosen arguments) is only partially resisted.

### [Where Do LLMs Decide to Break the Rules? Mechanistic Localization of Prompt Injection Compliance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37737)
**Authors:** Rui Wen, Jiayang Liu, Zeyu Yang, Jun Sakuma, Lu Sun | **Published:** 2026-09-29

Using layer-by-layer causal activation patching across five models (4B–32B), the authors find attack information is decodable from layer one but has causal leverage only at a **late-layer bottleneck** in the final third — patching it reverses compliance in 77–92% of cases. The compliance mechanism is a compact linear subspace (rank-8 to rank-64), and that same layer is the best site for attack detection.
