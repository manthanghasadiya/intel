## 🔐 SECURITY

### [📄 CVE-2026-112816: CVSS 10.0 in LXD and MicroCloud — impersonate a trusted client with only its public cert](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/critical-lxd-and-microcloud-flaw-cvss.html)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

LXD and MicroCloud validate mutual-TLS client certs by comparing *every* certificate in the submitted chain against the trust store — but TLS only proves possession of the leaf's private key. Attach a trusted client's public cert (not secret) behind your own leaf and the server accepts you as that client; if it's a privileged identity, you get root on the host. Affected: LXD 4.0.14, 5.0.10, 5.21.8, 6.9 and earlier. Fixed snaps: 6.10.1, 5.21.9, 5.0.11, 4.0.15; MicroCloud 3.4.1 / 2.1.5; Incus 7.6.0 (which found the bug). No known exploitation, not on KEV, no workaround beyond restricting network access to the API listener.

**Why it matters:** Certificate-chain confusion is the exact bug class that shows up in agent mTLS identity — "the cert is in the trust store" is not authentication. Add it to the identity-spoofing module.

### [📄 Polymarket loses $2.7M after its DebugBear script was swapped via a registrar-level DNS hijack](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-steal-about-27-million-from.html)
**⏱ Read time:** 4 min

On Oct 29 a malicious file was served from Polymarket's `cdn.debugbear[.]com` address. DebugBear's DNS was compromised at its registrar; the attacker targeted one customer. The script reused the signed-in session, found the order-placement and signer components, and either moved positions to the attacker's wallet (wallet logins) or sold them at worsening prices (email logins), then handed requests to Polymarket's relayer for submission. 28.6M POL from 33 wallets in 52 minutes (~$2.64M). Polymarket refunded 726 wallets in full and is paying out $3.9M total.

**Why it matters:** No keys were stolen — the attacker borrowed the *session's authority*. That is the shadow-agent threat model in production: whoever controls the page controls the signer.

### [📄 "SMS of Death" (CVE-2026-58882): one 8-byte text crashes Android's phone process, PoC published](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-android-sms-of-death-exploit-can-remotely-crash-unpatched-phones/)
**⏱ Read time:** 2 min

Improper header-length validation in `WapPushOverSms.decodeWapPdu` turns the length into −2147483648, raising `NegativeArraySizeException` and killing `com.android.phone` — zero-click, before the message ever hits the inbox, dropping live calls including emergency calls. Researcher Mark Blah says Google fixed it in the October 2026 updates for Android 14–17 without listing it in the bulletin; Android 12 and 13 remain unpatched. 20 messages put a test device into a ~90-second reboot loop requiring a factory reset.

**Why it matters:** Silent, unbulletin'd fixes mean your asset inventory can't tell you if you're patched. Flag Android 12/13 fleets as exposed and verify build levels by hand.

### [SEAL: Sealed Queries for Local Sanitization against Indirect Prompt Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39758)
**Authors:** Doohyun Kim, Donghwa Kang, Mingyu Jun, Brent ByungHoon Kang | **Published:** Oct 29, 2026 (announced Oct 30)

Learns a soft prompt plus scoring readout offline, then uses a frozen task model to delete attacker-controlled spans from tool output — reference stays independent of the inspected text, no verifier model, no defense-generated tokens. On AgentDojo it drops attack success from 34.6% to 0.1% while raising task-completion-without-success from 51.4% to 77.5%, using ~127× fewer defense tokens than PISanitizer.

**Full breakdown:** [papers/2026/11/seal-sealed-queries-local-sanitization.md](../../../papers/2026/11/seal-sealed-queries-local-sanitization.md)

### [Understanding and Mitigating Role Spoofing in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40396)
**Authors:** Ezra Edelman, Benjamin Edelman, Surbhi Goel, Eran Malach | **Published:** Oct 29, 2026 (announced Oct 30)

Shows a malicious tool output can impersonate the *user* by imitating both stylistic and structural cues (control tokens, formatting). Adversarial training against content-based injections leaves structural attacks working — which explains why hardened frontier models still fall — and linear probes trained to predict speaker roles detect the spoof.

## 🔥 HOT TAKE + CONTENT IDEA
