## 🤖 AI NEWS

### [📄 NVIDIA opens GR00T 2 — a world-action model, not a VLA — plus Isaac Lab 3.0 at CoRL](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-2/)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

GR00T 2 predicts future video frames *and* the actions that produce them, so the model learns how physical interactions unfold rather than mapping observation→action directly. The payoff is cross-embodiment transfer: a two-handed robot that only ever saw human video lines a lab funnel with filter paper in 65% of trials; a YAM arm shown only handheld UMI-gripper footage assembles a toy dump truck end-to-end in 60%; 40% zero-shot on 50 tasks never seen on the target robot, rising to 95% after five demos and a 30-minute fine-tune on a single RTX 5090. GR00T 2 Turbo runs whole-body loco-manipulation in 124 ms per prediction on the Unitree G1's own Jetson Thor. Paper, code and weights "available in December."

**Why it matters:** A reasoning model can now call GR00T 2 through a WAM API and hand it what-and-where instructions (92% instruction-following vs 40% baseline). That is an embodied agent loop with a network-facing control surface — the same trust questions you already apply to tool-calling agents, but with actuators.

### [📄 Google adds TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max — confidential inference gets its own silicon](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/google-broadens-its-ai-hardware-portfolio-with-two-new-specialized-chips/)
**⏱ Read time:** 2 min

Two variants of the eighth-gen TPU line: **TPU 8tx**, built with AMD, puts security "into the hardware layer, keeping sensitive data and model weights protected and isolated even while in active use," and carries a custom inference network so reasoning can run alongside training (useful for distilling small models on data that never leaves the device). **TPU 8i Max** bonds two inference chips into one package for double memory, bandwidth and context capacity.

**Why it matters:** Confidential-computing hardware is becoming the default substrate for regulated inference. That shifts a chunk of your threat model from "attack the model host" to "attack the enclave's software stack" — exactly the gap NEAR AI hit this week (see Security).

### [📄 New York's RAISE Act switches on: frontier labs must register by January 1](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-anthropic-face-new-oversight-as-new-yorks-ai-safety-law-takes-effect/)
**⏱ Read time:** 2 min

New York opened the registration portal Tuesday. Developers with more than $500M in annual revenue must register by 2027-01-01, publish safety frameworks, report critical safety incidents within 72 hours, and issue public model transparency reports. Enforcement runs through a new state office (DIGIT); penalties are $1M for a first violation and $3M thereafter. Hochul also stood up the NY Center for AI Responsibility and Research at Binghamton with $55M.

**Why it matters:** First US state regime with real per-violation money and an incident-reporting clock. "Critical safety incident" definitions are where agent-caused damage (autonomous exploitation, agent-driven data exfil) will eventually get classified — worth tracking the rulemaking text, not the headlines.

### [📄 Anthropic ships Claude Meetings — the agent joins the call, not just the notes](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-launches-claude-meetings-to-turn-workplace-discussions-into-plans-and-tasks/)
**⏱ Read time:** 2 min

Claude joins a Google Meet call as a visible participant, drafts the plan being discussed in real time, builds timelines on a whiteboard, answers in chat, and pulls from connected work apps ("what were last year's numbers") — output lands in claude.ai after the call. Beta on Team and Enterprise. It will not join calls with external guests unless an admin authorizes it, and auto-leaves if a guest joins. Ships alongside Claude Whiteboards and Claude Sheets.

## 🔐 SECURITY

### [📄 Microsoft patches 1,259 flaws; exploited Outlook bug leaks NTLMv2 hashes (CVE-2026-124194)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/microsoft-patches-1259-flaws-including.html)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

Second consecutive 1,000+ month: 726 Windows, 174 Office, 59 SQL Server, 27 SharePoint, 165 non-Microsoft CVE IDs, over 5,100 CVEs fixed this year. The headline is a **Moderate (6.5)** Outlook spoofing flaw: a crafted message file makes Outlook reach an attacker-controlled network location **without** showing the usual external-content warning, disclosing an NTLMv2 hash that can be replayed to impersonate the user. Microsoft flags it as exploited; CISA added it to KEV with a November 24 federal deadline. Credited to researcher Nono and Anthropic's Benoît Sevens (using Claude). Also fixed: RCEs in Windows Search (CVE-2026-116928), LDAP (CVE-2026-119958), Schannel (CVE-2026-119952), plus an **Azure OpenAI privilege escalation (CVE-2026-119990)**.

**Why it matters:** Severity labels are lying to you again — one click, no prompt, hash stolen. Prioritize by "is it exposed and is it being exploited," not by CVSS. The Azure OpenAI EoP is the one to read if you run model endpoints in Azure.

### [📄 Claroc finds 35,578 Grok-built apps open to anyone — including 24 built by X and SpaceXSI staff](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/over-35000-grok-built-apps-exposed.html)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

Of 37,571 grok.me apps discovered via subdomain enumeration and passive DNS, 35,578 required no sign-in. Roughly 1 in 10 held internal business or personal data; ~2% rated high or critical. The 24 staff apps included a confidential onboarding plan for a new sales hire, a sales-territory map of 594 accounts, and an ad proposal to a US federal agency marked confidential. Beyond that: 30 identifiable organizations, 26 with >1,000 employees, 6 Fortune 500 — a hospital group's searchable 3,500-employee list, an ICU briefing on a single patient, a fire department's live shift board, and a contractor timesheet whose source contained every employee password in plaintext. The root cause is a default: publishing pre-selects **"Anyone with the link,"** and subdomain names leak through DNS lookups even though Grok suggests four random words.

**Why it matters:** Vibe-coded apps are now an unmanaged shadow-SaaS tier with guessable-but-indexed hostnames. Two of the researchers' own test apps also showed Grok-written auth that added each signer-in to the team *before* checking membership — the check always passed.

### [📄 Railway: stolen customer AI keys bought an estimated $740,000 of inference](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/railway-says-stolen-customer-ai-keys.html)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

Entry was a build-system escape: at 07:28 UTC on October 20 an attacker used a previously unknown flaw to partly break filesystem isolation around a build and read files on the host VM, obtaining a credential build servers use to push images. The on-call engineer was paged 6.5 hours later. The attacker then decrypted environment variables in at most 1.85% of workspaces (~100,000 upper bound) and their queries targeted **AI provider keys** specifically. Also taken: linked-account tokens for 1,852 users (BYOK agent keys, GitHub OAuth — all reset), and the stored secret histories of 353 builds. Refund matrix differs by provider: AWS Bedrock and Gemini cover fraud, OpenAI and SpaceXSI review case-by-case, Anthropic is still negotiating (Railway is covering in the meantime), OpenRouter's ToS forbids refunds so Railway eats it.

**Why it matters:** LLMjacking has a measured price now: ~$740K from one PaaS tenant population, in one incident, from keys sitting in env vars. If your agent stack keeps provider keys in a PaaS/host environment, rotate before October 21 and treat build-time isolation as production attack surface.

### [📄 SGLang RCE lands on NEAR AI's inference servers — TEE did not help](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/sglang-rce-exploit-breaches-near-ai.html)
**⏱ Read time:** 2 min

An attacker exploited an RCE in the SGLang inference engine to run code on a NEAR AI inference server, and could have observed requests processed that day — **including requests sent in confidential mode**. NEAR AI's own wording is the lesson: a TEE "does not protect against a vulnerability in the software running inside the environment," and SGLang runs inside the TEE. The attacker then reached a deprecated internal system behind Private Chat (account details for ~100 users) and 18 agents on an older, non-TEE deployment (chat history, settings, logs). Two customers' API keys were exposed in logs. No CVE, no affected-version list, no confirmed upstream fix yet.

**Why it matters:** Confidential computing buys you host-level isolation and nothing else. Attestation tells you what code is running; it says nothing about whether that code has an RCE. Treat the TEE image's dependency tree as the actual boundary.

## 🔌 MCP / AI AGENTS

### [📄 Microsoft's "Lazarus": backdoor an agent model by editing fewer than 10 neurons](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/microsoft-researchers-show-ai-agents.html)
**⏱ Read time:** 3 min

Instead of poisoned training data, the researchers directly compute settings for a handful of rarely-firing neurons in the last feed-forward layer, so each edited neuron forces one token of an attacker command into the output when a trigger string appears — in the user's request *or* in a tool's returned output. On Nemotron-Terminal-8B they edited **7 of 12,000+ neurons** in that layer using 23 example sessions: ≥98.8% of triggered sessions emitted the command, none of the trigger-free ones did. Tuned edits hit 51–99.5% on Qwen3.6-27B (6 neurons) and Gemma-4-31B-IT. Capability barely moves: Terminal-Bench 46.59% → 42.05%, AIME mostly intact. A detector trained on this edit style caught 91.4% of edited checkpoints with a 9.7% false-positive rate (35 edited / 31 clean — small). Microsoft credits Claude Code on Opus 4.5 with the core idea and says no threat actors are using it yet.

**Why it matters:** An 8B agent model with a 10-neuron backdoor runs `curl | bash` as root in a container three turns into a normal debugging session. Anyone who can touch a checkpoint before deployment — insider, stolen account, build pipeline, public hub upload — is now an agent-model supply-chain actor. Defense is checkpoint diffing against upstream weights, not evaluation suites.

## 📄 PAPERS

### [From Attack Surface to Defense Architecture: Compositional Security for Agentic Coding Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13960)
**Authors:** Simon Hegelich, Bardh Prenkaj, Habiba Sarhan, Morteza Shahrezaye, Kolja Hegelich, Christian Montag, Ralf Schneider, Erik Cambria | **Published:** 2026-11-10

Argues a coding harness is structurally an automated living-off-the-land platform — once injected instructions land in retrieved content, `bash`, `curl` and `find` are the payload — and that security must come from permission composition, not model behavior. Presents ActiveSecHarness: Macaroon-style HMAC capability tokens across four trust rings, a 2D product taint lattice (trust × sensitivity) with a workspace provenance ledger, and deterministic Ring-0 OS guards. Ring 0 hits ABR 1.000 / FPR 0.000 in 12.7 µs where a local LLM judge (qwen3:4b) refuses 98.3% of *benign* work at 142.3 ms.

### [BypassGym: A Benchmark for Evaluating AI Agents' Endpoint Defense Evasion Capabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13684)
**Authors:** Weifeng Yuan, Zifan Chen, Yucheng Zhang, Feng Dong, Peng Gao, Haoyu Wang | **Published:** 2026-11-10

First benchmark for the one offensive capability nobody measured: can an agent write a loader that executes C2 shellcode on a real endpoint without EDR blocking or killing it. Runs real Windows VMs and six EDR products on Proxmox VE and feeds structured detection results back to the agent. Even lightweight models win — DeepSeek-V4.1 Flash passed 17 of 18 runs; Bitdefender blocked the most and still let 5 of 15 through.

**Full breakdown:** [papers/2026/11/bypassgym-endpoint-evasion.md](../../../papers/2026/11/bypassgym-endpoint-evasion.md)

### [Isolation Is Not Confidentiality: Security Boundaries of Geometric Access Control in RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14061)
**Authors:** Steve Nouyep, Maxime Puys, Sébastien Salva | **Published:** 2026-11-10

Takes the "search-preserving geometric access control" family in RAG vector DBs and separates four properties everyone conflates: policy isolation, representation confidentiality, query forgery resistance, policy-membership hiding. Across three encoders, geometric transformations keep retrieval utility and give strong policy isolation — and fail the other three. A curious user recovers original embeddings from a few hundred aligned pairs and forges policy-compatible queries; policy-dependent geometry leaks policy membership even when unauthorized retrieval is blocked.

**Full breakdown:** [papers/2026/11/rag-geometric-access-control.md](../../../papers/2026/11/rag-geometric-access-control.md)

## 🔥 HOT TAKE + CONTENT IDEA

**Three of today's four big stories are the same bug: a safe default that wasn't.** Grok pre-selected "Anyone with the link" and leaked 35,578 apps; Grok-written auth added the user to the team *before* checking membership; NEAR AI put SGLang inside a TEE and inherited every bug in the image; Microsoft shipped an Outlook path that silently skips its own external-content warning. The model was never the weak link — the ordering of checks and the state of the default were.

Concrete piece for today: **"Defaults Are the Attack Surface."** Take the Claroc test — publish two throwaway Grok apps, one contact list and one 4-person customer list — and record what the generated code does with an uninvited Google account. Screencap the insert-before-authorize ordering and post it with a one-line fix (authorize, then insert; default to "Only you"; scope by email domain). That is a 5-minute video with a reproducible demo, a vendor-confirmed finding, and a one-line mitigation — three things your audience can act on before their next vibe-coded internal app ships.
